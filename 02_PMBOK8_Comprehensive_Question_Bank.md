### Answers — Part 1 (Foundations, Value Delivery and Principles)

**Q1 — Answer: C**

*Correct:* C. Projects are temporary and produce unique results; operations are ongoing and repetitive

Projects are temporary endeavours that create unique products, services or results; operations sustain the business through repetitive work. Projects usually change operations.

**Q2 — Answer: B**

*Correct:* B. A program

Related projects coordinated to achieve benefits (reduced congestion) that would not be achieved by managing them separately form a program.

**Q3 — Answer: A**

*Correct:* A. Outputs are what is produced; outcomes result from using them; benefits are the gains that follow

Activities create outputs; using the outputs creates outcomes; outcomes produce benefits; together they contribute to value.

**Q4 — Answer: D**

*Correct:* D. Selecting and prioritising investments to achieve strategic objectives

Portfolios align investments with strategy, balance risk and allocate resources. Detailed scheduling and change approval occur at project level.

**Q5 — Answer: C**

*Correct:* C. The organisation's geographic distribution of facilities and staff

Geographic distribution of facilities is internal to the organisation and not under the team's immediate control. Industry standards, interest rates and weather are external.

**Q6 — Answer: B**

*Correct:* B. Organisational process assets

Templates, historical information and knowledge repositories belong to the performing organisation and are OPAs.

**Q7 — Answer: D**

*Correct:* D. The project manager has moderate to high authority and is assigned full-time

In a strong matrix, PM authority and resource availability are moderate to high, and the PM is full-time. Team members still have functional managers.

**Q8 — Answer: C**

*Correct:* C. The sponsor or project initiator

The charter is issued by someone external to the project at a level able to fund and authorise it — usually the sponsor.

**Q9 — Answer: A, D**

*Correct:* A. Analysis of the business need and recommended option / D. Cost-benefit analysis with financial measures such as NPV

A business case justifies the investment: it describes the need, analyses options, recommends one and includes cost-benefit analysis. Activity durations and team artifacts belong elsewhere.

**Q10 — Answer: B**

*Correct:* B. Many benefits appear only after delivery, so measuring them confirms the value created

Value is often realised after the project closes. The benefit owner tracks benefits to confirm the business case and inform future decisions.

**Q11 — Answer: A**

*Correct:* A. Integrate sustainability within all project areas

Considering long-term environmental and economic impacts over the product life cycle reflects sustainability, while also reflecting a holistic view.

**Q12 — Answer: C**

*Correct:* C. Adopt a holistic view

A holistic view considers the project as part of a larger system, including shared resources and other initiatives.

**Q13 — Answer: B**

*Correct:* B. Inviting people to raise concerns and letting the team decide within agreed boundaries

Empowered cultures rely on psychological safety, trust and delegated decision-making with clear boundaries.

**Q14 — Answer: D**

*Correct:* D. Embed quality — quality means meeting requirements and fitness for use, not extra features

Gold plating is not quality. Quality is defined by requirements and real stakeholder needs; extra features add cost and risk without agreed value.

**Q15 — Answer: D**

*Correct:* D. Six in the Eighth Edition; twelve in the Seventh Edition

The Eighth Edition consolidated the Seventh Edition's twelve principles into six.

**Q16 — Answer: D**

*Correct:* D. Business Acumen

Business Acumen connects projects to strategy and value through knowledge of the business, finance, markets and regulations.

**Q17 — Answer: A**

*Correct:* A. It describes good practice that teams adapt to their context instead of mandating procedures

The Guide describes what is generally good practice; the team tailors processes, methods and artifacts to the context.

**Q18 — Answer: A**

*Correct:* A. Regulatory, legal or social requirement

Projects initiated to comply with new laws or regulations fall under regulatory, legal or social requirements.

**Q19 — Answer: D**

*Correct:* D. The portfolio manager or portfolio governance board

Resource conflicts across projects that affect strategy are resolved at portfolio level, which prioritises investments.

**Q20 — Answer: 1→D, 2→B, 3→A, 4→C**

*Correct pairs:* 1. Program → Related components coordinated to obtain benefits; 2. Portfolio → Components managed as a group to achieve strategic objectives; 3. Operations → Ongoing work that sustains the organisation; 4. Product → An artifact that may be created or changed by several projects over its life cycle

Programs coordinate for benefits; portfolios align investments to strategy; operations sustain the business; products persist over a life cycle that can span many projects.

**Q21 — Answer: D**

*Correct:* D. Stop using the materials, inform the right stakeholders and source a compliant alternative

Accountable leadership, sustainability and compliance require transparency and adherence to laws. Cost savings never justify illegal or unethical actions.

**Q22 — Answer: C**

*Correct:* C. Benefits management plan

The benefits management plan defines benefits, metrics, timeframe, benefit owner, assumptions and risks.

**Q23 — Answer: C**

*Correct:* C. Leadership needs accurate information to adjust strategy and investments

The value delivery system depends on feedback loops; inaccurate status prevents good portfolio and strategic decisions.

**Q24 — Answer: A**

*Correct:* A. Bringing plans, work, people and changes together into a coherent whole

Integration is the PM's core role: aligning processes, knowledge, context and complexity to deliver outcomes.

**Q25 — Answer: A**

*Correct:* A. Value can be tangible or intangible and may be realised during or after the project

Value includes financial and non-financial benefits such as reputation or public good, and is often realised progressively or after closure.

### Answers — Part 2 (Life Cycles, Focus Areas and Processes)

**Q26 — Answer: C**

*Correct:* C. Iterative development with a single delivery

Repeated refinement through prototypes is iterative; delivering everything at once is a single delivery.

**Q27 — Answer: C**

*Correct:* C. Planning

Planning has 19 of the 40 processes, Monitoring and Controlling 10, Executing 8, Initiating 2 and Closing 1.

**Q28 — Answer: D**

*Correct:* D. Implement Risk Responses

Implement Risk Responses is the only executing process in the Risk domain. Analysis and response planning are planning processes; Monitor Risks is monitoring and controlling.

**Q29 — Answer: C**

*Correct:* C. Validate Scope

Validate Scope (formal acceptance) and Monitor and Control Scope are the two monitoring and controlling processes of the Scope domain.

**Q30 — Answer: A**

*Correct:* A. Governance

Plan Sourcing Strategy is a Governance planning process, reflecting that sourcing decisions are strategic. Acquisition and control of resources are in the Resources domain.

**Q31 — Answer: C**

*Correct:* C. Manage Project Execution

Manage Project Execution in the Governance domain is the renamed Direct and Manage Project Work.

**Q32 — Answer: A**

*Correct:* A. Continue with modifications

Phase gate decisions can be to continue, continue with modifications, repeat the phase or end the project.

**Q33 — Answer: B, E**

*Correct:* B. The issue log can be updated without a formal change request / E. Changes to the schedule baseline require formal change control

Project documents such as the issue log are updated as needed; baselines and the plan require change control. Registers are not baseline components.

**Q34 — Answer: B**

*Correct:* B. Assumption log

The charter and the assumption log are the key outputs of initiating the project or phase. The risk register is created in Identify Risks.

**Q35 — Answer: C**

*Correct:* C. Work performance information

Data that has been analysed against the plan becomes work performance information.

**Q36 — Answer: A**

*Correct:* A. Predictive

Predictive approaches suit stable requirements and expensive change, such as construction.

**Q37 — Answer: A**

*Correct:* A. Issue log

The issue log is a project document. Baselines, subsidiary plans and the change management plan are plan components.

**Q38 — Answer: B**

*Correct:* B. Within each iteration, repeatedly throughout the project

Planning, executing, monitoring and controlling, and closing occur in every iteration; initiating recurs with new releases or phases.

**Q39 — Answer: B**

*Correct:* B. Perform Qualitative Risk Analysis and Perform Quantitative Risk Analysis

The two analysis processes were combined into a single process covering both qualitative and quantitative techniques.

**Q40 — Answer: D**

*Correct:* D. Periodic delivery

Fixed, regular releases are periodic. Continuous delivery releases whenever features are ready.

**Q41 — Answer: B**

*Correct:* B. Integrate and Align Project Plans

Integrate and Align Project Plans is the Eighth Edition name for developing the project management plan.

**Q42 — Answer: A**

*Correct:* A. Confusion (disorder)

Cynefin's central domain, confusion, applies when it is not yet known which domain the situation belongs to.

**Q43 — Answer: 1→D, 2→A, 3→C, 4→B**

*Correct pairs:* 1. Identify Stakeholders → Initiating; 2. Develop Budget → Planning; 3. Lead the Team → Executing; 4. Monitor Communications → Monitoring and Controlling

Identify Stakeholders is initiating, Develop Budget planning, Lead the Team executing, and Monitor Communications monitoring and controlling.

**Q44 — Answer: B**

*Correct:* B. Defect repair

Defect repair modifies a non-conforming product. Corrective and preventive actions address performance; updates change controlled documents.

**Q45 — Answer: B**

*Correct:* B. To align everyone on objectives, roles and the plan as the team moves into execution

The kickoff aligns stakeholders and the team on objectives, roles and the plan, typically at the end of planning or the start of execution.
