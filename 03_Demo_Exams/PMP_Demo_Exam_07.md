**How to take this exam:** set a timer for 240 minutes, sit somewhere quiet, and answer every question without notes. Write your answers on paper (question number + letter). There is no penalty for guessing, so never leave a question blank. Pace yourself at about **80 seconds per question** — you should be near Q60 at 80 minutes and Q120 at 160 minutes. Only after finishing, check the [Answer Key](#answer-key) and read the [explanations](#explanations), then complete the [score analysis](#score-analysis).

*Question types:* most questions have one best answer. Multiple-response questions say how many options to choose. Matching questions ask you to pair items with options. Fill-in-the-blank questions need a number. Case-study sets share one scenario across several questions.

> #### Case Study — Sentinel flight simulator programme
>
> Sentinel Systems is the prime contractor delivering flight training simulators to a national air force. The hardware (cockpit replicas and motion platforms) follows a predictive plan with formal design reviews; agile teams develop the simulation software.
>
> - The contract requires everyone working with **classified flight data** to hold a security clearance, and the data may be processed **only on accredited systems** at Sentinel's secure site.
> - The manufacturer of a key **graphics processor** used in the visual system will **discontinue it in 18 months**. The simulators must be supported for 15 years.
> - Instructors who currently train pilots in real aircraft doubt that simulators can replace real flying hours.
> - A new government has announced a **defence spending review** that could cut the programme's budget by 15%.
>
