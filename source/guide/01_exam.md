# Chapter 1 — The PMP® Exam (2026) and How the PMBOK® Guide Fits

**After this chapter you should be able to:**
- describe the format of the PMP® exam that has been delivered since July 2026;
- list the three exam domains, their weights and the 26 tasks;
- explain the relationship between the exam content outline and the *PMBOK® Guide – Eighth Edition*;
- plan your preparation and know what "good enough" looks like on practice exams.

## 1.1 Which certification does this guide target?

PMI offers several certifications. The two that rely most heavily on the PMBOK® Guide are:

| | **PMP® (Project Management Professional)** | **CAPM® (Certified Associate in Project Management)** |
|---|---|---|
| Who it is for | Practitioners who have led projects | Entry-level and aspiring practitioners |
| Experience required | Yes (see 1.6) | No |
| Question style | Mostly situational ("What should the project manager do next?") | More knowledge-based ("Which artifact…?") |
| Based on | PMP Examination Content Outline (ECO) | CAPM Examination Content Outline |

This guide is written for the **PMP**, because it is the certification most people mean when they study the PMBOK® Guide. Everything you need for the CAPM is also here: the CAPM tests the same vocabulary, processes, life cycles, agile frameworks and business-analysis basics, only at a lower level of difficulty.

## 1.2 Exam format (from 9 July 2026)

PMI introduced a new PMP Examination Content Outline (ECO) in July 2026. The most widely reported format is:

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| Number of questions | **180** — of which **170 are scored** and **10 are unscored pretest items** (you cannot tell which ones) |
| Time | **240 minutes** |
| Breaks | Two optional 10-minute breaks. Once you start a break you cannot go back to the previous block of questions |
| Delivery | Pearson VUE test centre or online proctored |
| Item types | Multiple choice (one answer), multiple response (choose two or three), matching / drag-and-drop, hotspot (click on part of a graphic), limited fill-in-the-blank (usually a calculation), and **case-study sets** in which several questions share one scenario that may include tables or charts |
| Approaches | About **40% predictive**, about **60% agile or hybrid** |

> **Exam tip.** Pace yourself at roughly **80 seconds per question**. With 240 minutes for 180 questions you have 1.33 minutes per question. A good checkpoint plan is: question 60 at about 80 minutes, question 120 at about 160 minutes, leaving a few minutes at the end to revisit flagged items.

> **Trap.** Some websites still show the pre-2026 format (230 minutes, 175 scored + 5 unscored questions). Always check the current PMP Certification Handbook on pmi.org.

## 1.3 The three domains

The exam is organised into three **domains** (areas of practice). The 2026 outline moved a lot of weight from People and Process into the Business Environment:

| Domain | 2021 outline | **2026 outline** | What it covers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **People** | 42% | **33%** | Leading and developing the team, conflict, stakeholder engagement and expectations, communication, knowledge transfer |
| **Process** | 50% | **41%** | Planning and managing scope, schedule, finance, resources, procurement, quality, value delivery, status and closure |
| **Business Environment** | 8% | **26%** | Governance, compliance, change control, impediments and issues, risk, continuous improvement, organisational change, external environment |

On 180 questions this is roughly **59 People, 74 Process and 47 Business Environment** questions.

## 1.4 The 26 tasks

Each domain contains tasks. Each task has **enablers** — examples of the work involved. You do not need to memorise task numbers, but you should know *where* a topic lives, because it tells you what the question is really testing.

### Domain I — People (33%)

| # | Task | What questions typically test |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Develop a common vision | Helping the team and stakeholders share one understanding of the goal: vision statements, project/product vision, team charter, alignment workshops |
| 2 | Manage conflicts | Recognising the source and stage of conflict, choosing an appropriate resolution approach, facilitating rather than imposing |
| 3 | Lead the project team | Servant leadership, empowerment, motivation, coaching, team development, psychological safety, performance |
| 4 | Engage stakeholders | Identifying and analysing stakeholders, building relationships, choosing engagement strategies |
| 5 | Align stakeholder expectations | Surfacing expectations early, facilitating agreement among stakeholders with different interests |
| 6 | Manage stakeholder expectations | Monitoring satisfaction, adjusting engagement, keeping expectations consistent with what can be delivered |
| 7 | Help ensure knowledge transfer | Lessons learned, documentation, mentoring, handovers, transfer of knowledge to operations and to future projects |
| 8 | Plan and manage communication | Communication requirements, methods, frequency, channels, feedback, information radiators, reporting |

### Domain II — Process (41%)

| # | Task | What questions typically test |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Develop an integrated project management plan and plan delivery | Choosing the approach, integrating subsidiary plans, planning releases and iterations |
| 2 | Develop and manage project scope | Requirements, scope statement, WBS/backlog, validating deliverables, preventing scope creep |
| 3 | Help ensure value-based delivery | Identifying value with stakeholders, prioritising by value, incremental delivery, measuring benefits |
| 4 | Plan and manage resources | Team and physical resources, acquisition, allocation, resource constraints |
| 5 | Plan and manage procurement | Make-or-buy, contract types, selecting sellers, administering contracts |
| 6 | Plan and manage finance | Estimates, budgets, reserves, earned value, forecasting, tracking spend |
| 7 | Plan and optimise quality of products/deliverables | Quality planning, acceptance criteria, definition of done, quality assurance and control |
| 8 | Plan and manage schedule | Estimating, sequencing, critical path, compression, release planning, velocity |
| 9 | Evaluate project status | Performance measurement, metrics, dashboards, reporting, forecasts |
| 10 | Manage project closure | Formal acceptance, transition, contract closure, lessons learned, archiving, releasing resources |

### Domain III — Business Environment (26%)

| # | Task | What questions typically test |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Define and establish project governance | Decision rights, escalation paths, phase gates, roles (sponsor, steering committee, PMO, CCB) |
| 2 | Plan and manage project compliance | Regulatory, legal, contractual and organisational requirements; audits; consequences of non-compliance |
| 3 | Manage and control changes | Integrated change control, change requests, the change control board, backlog-based change in agile |
| 4 | Remove impediments and manage issues | Issue logs, escalation, servant-leader impediment removal |
| 5 | Plan and manage risk | Risk planning, identification, analysis, responses, monitoring, reserves |
| 6 | Continuous improvement | Retrospectives, lessons learned, process improvement, Kaizen, PDCA |
| 7 | Support organisational change | Readiness, change management models, adoption, resistance, transition to operations |
| 8 | Evaluate external business environment changes | Regulations, market, technology, economic and social changes and their effect on the project |

> **What changed.** Risk and change control now sit in the **Business Environment** domain, and the **Process** domain has separate tasks for *finance*, *resources* and *procurement*. Expect more questions on governance, compliance, sustainability, artificial intelligence and organisational change than in older practice tests.

## 1.5 How the PMBOK® Guide relates to the exam

The PMP exam is built from the **Examination Content Outline**, not from a single book. PMI uses several references when it writes questions, and the *PMBOK® Guide – Eighth Edition* is the core one. Questions rarely ask you to recite the Guide. Instead they describe a situation and ask what a competent project manager would do. To answer them you need three things:

1. **Vocabulary and concepts** from the PMBOK® Guide (principles, performance domains, processes, artifacts, techniques).
2. **Judgment** — knowing which action comes *first*, which is *best* and which is merely possible.
3. **Context awareness** — recognising whether the scenario is predictive, agile or hybrid, because the right action can differ.

## 1.6 Eligibility and scoring (summary)

To apply for the PMP you need **35 contact hours** of project management education (or an active CAPM) and experience leading projects within the last eight years:

- **36 months** with a four-year (bachelor's) degree or global equivalent;
- **60 months** with a high-school diploma or associate's degree;
- **24 months** with a bachelor's or postgraduate degree from a GAC-accredited programme.

Your application may be selected for **audit**, in which case you must provide evidence (diplomas, training certificates, supervisor signatures).

**Scoring.** PMI does not publish a fixed pass mark. A panel of experts determines the passing standard using psychometric methods, and your report shows **Pass/Fail** plus a rating per domain: **Above Target, Target, Below Target, Needs Improvement**. As a practical rule, if you consistently score **75% or more** on good-quality full-length practice exams, you are in a strong position.

## 1.7 What the exam rewards — a first look at the "PMI mindset"

You will see this in detail in Chapter 21. In short, the best answer usually:

- **analyses before acting** — understand the situation, check the plan, assess the impact;
- **addresses the root cause** rather than a symptom;
- **involves the right people** — talk directly to the person concerned, collaborate with the team, engage the sponsor or product owner when it is their decision;
- **follows the agreed process** (for example, a change request in predictive work, backlog prioritisation by the product owner in agile work);
- **serves the team** — remove impediments, coach, protect focus, empower decisions;
- **keeps value, ethics and sustainability in view.**

Answers that ignore a problem, escalate too early, blame people, bypass governance or let the project manager make decisions that belong to others are usually wrong.
