# Chapter 12 — Stakeholders Performance Domain (including Communications)

**After this chapter you should be able to:**
- identify, analyse and prioritise stakeholders using common models;
- plan, manage and monitor stakeholder engagement, including expectation alignment;
- plan, manage and monitor communications, and calculate communication channels;
- choose communication methods (interactive, push, pull) and technologies appropriate to the audience;
- engage stakeholders effectively in predictive, adaptive and hybrid projects.

## 12.1 What the Stakeholders domain covers

The Stakeholders domain addresses **identifying, understanding, engaging and communicating with the people and organisations who affect or are affected by the project**. In the Eighth Edition it contains both stakeholder engagement and communications (seven processes):

| Focus area | Processes |
|---|---|
| Initiating | **Identify Stakeholders** |
| Planning | **Plan Stakeholder Engagement**, **Plan Communications Management** |
| Executing | **Manage Stakeholder Engagement**, **Manage Communications** |
| Monitoring and Controlling | **Monitor Stakeholder Engagement**, **Monitor Communications** |

**Desired outcomes:** a productive working relationship with stakeholders; agreement on objectives; stakeholders who are supporters are satisfied; opposition does not undermine the project; information reaches the right people, at the right time, in the right format; expectations are aligned with what will be delivered.

> **Exam tip.** Stakeholder engagement is **continuous**. Identify stakeholders early and **re-identify** them throughout (at each phase, when the organisation changes, when someone new appears). A stakeholder who "suddenly appears" in the middle of a project is a sign that identification was incomplete — add them to the register and analyse them.

## 12.2 Identify Stakeholders *(Initiating)*

**Purpose:** identify stakeholders regularly and analyse their interests, involvement, interdependencies, influence and potential impact on success.

- **Inputs:** charter, business documents, agreements, EEFs, OPAs.
- **Techniques:** brainstorming, interviews, questionnaires and surveys, **stakeholder analysis**, document analysis, **data representation** (stakeholder mapping).
- **Output:** the **stakeholder register** — identification information (name, position, contact, role), **assessment information** (major requirements, expectations, potential for influence, phase of most interest), **stakeholder classification** (internal/external, impact, influence, power, interest, direction of influence).

### Stakeholder analysis models

| Model | Dimensions | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Power/interest grid** | Power (authority) × interest (concern) | High power–high interest: **manage closely**; high power–low interest: **keep satisfied**; low power–high interest: **keep informed**; low power–low interest: **monitor** |
| **Power/influence grid** | Authority × active involvement | Similar quadrants |
| **Impact/influence grid** | Ability to cause changes × active involvement | Similar quadrants |
| **Salience model** | **Power** (ability to impose will), **urgency** (need for immediate attention — time-sensitive or high-stakes), **legitimacy** (appropriateness of involvement) | Stakeholders with all three ("definitive") get most attention; useful for large, complex communities |
| **Stakeholder cube** | Combines grid dimensions in 3-D | Refines classification |
| **Directions of influence** | **Upward** (senior management, sponsor), **downward** (team, specialists), **outward** (suppliers, regulators, public, customers), **sideward** (peers, other PMs) | Tailor communications and engagement |
| **Prioritisation** | Rank by impact, interest, power | When there are many stakeholders |

## 12.3 Plan Stakeholder Engagement *(Planning)*

**Purpose:** develop approaches to involve stakeholders based on their needs, expectations, interests and potential impact.

### Stakeholder engagement assessment matrix

Compares **current (C)** and **desired (D)** engagement levels:

| Stakeholder | Unaware | Resistant | Neutral | Supportive | Leading |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Finance director | | C | | D | |
| Union representative | C | | D | | |
| Head of sales | | | | C | D |

- **Unaware** — not aware of the project and its potential impacts.
- **Resistant** — aware but resistant to the project or its changes.
- **Neutral** — aware but neither supportive nor resistant.
- **Supportive** — aware and supportive of the work and its outcomes.
- **Leading** — aware and actively engaged in ensuring success.

The gap between C and D drives engagement actions. Not every stakeholder needs to be "leading" — the desired level depends on their role.

**Output:** the **stakeholder engagement plan** — strategies and actions for productive involvement; may be formal or informal, detailed or broad. It can contain sensitive information (e.g., notes about resistant stakeholders), so distribution must be handled carefully.

## 12.4 Manage Stakeholder Engagement *(Executing)*

**Purpose:** communicate and work with stakeholders to meet their needs and expectations, address issues and foster appropriate involvement.

- Engage stakeholders at the right stages; manage expectations through negotiation and communication; address risks and concerns; clarify and resolve issues.
- **Techniques:** communication skills (feedback), **interpersonal skills** (conflict management, cultural awareness, negotiation, observation/conversation, political awareness), ground rules, meetings.
- **Outputs:** change requests, issue log updates, updates to the stakeholder register and engagement plan.

### Aligning and managing expectations (PMP People tasks 5 and 6)

- **Surface expectations early** — interviews, workshops, product vision sessions; record them (stakeholder register, requirements, assumptions).
- **Find common ground** — facilitate workshops; use objective criteria (business case, value, strategy).
- **Make trade-offs explicit** — show the impact of competing demands on scope, schedule, cost and risk.
- **Confirm agreement** — documented decisions, acceptance criteria, working agreements.
- **Monitor satisfaction** — feedback, surveys, demos, informal conversations.
- **Reset expectations transparently** when reality changes — early, honestly, with options.

## 12.5 Monitor Stakeholder Engagement *(Monitoring and Controlling)*

**Purpose:** monitor relationships and tailor engagement strategies. Techniques: alternatives analysis, root-cause analysis, stakeholder analysis, **engagement assessment matrix** (has C moved toward D?), feedback, active listening, cultural awareness, leadership, networking, political awareness, meetings. Outputs: work performance information, change requests, updates.

## 12.6 Plan Communications Management *(Planning)*

**Purpose:** develop an appropriate approach and plan for communication activities based on the information needs of each stakeholder or group, available assets and the needs of the project.

### Communication requirements analysis

Determine the information needs of stakeholders: who needs what information, when, how often, in what format, who provides it, and why. Consider organisational charts, stakeholder responsibilities, disciplines involved, logistics (locations, time zones), internal and external information needs, legal requirements.

### Number of communication channels

**Channels = n (n − 1) ÷ 2**, where n = number of people (including the PM).

*Example:* a team of 10 has 10 × 9 ÷ 2 = **45** channels. If it grows to 15, there are 15 × 14 ÷ 2 = 105 channels — **60 more**. Communication complexity grows much faster than team size.

### Communication models

**Basic sender–receiver model:** the **sender** encodes the message (thoughts → words, symbols) and transmits it through a **medium/channel**; **noise** (distance, technology, culture, language, distractions) may interfere; the **receiver** decodes it and **acknowledges** receipt; then provides **feedback/response**. The sender is responsible for clear encoding and for confirming the message was understood; the receiver for decoding completely and acknowledging.

**Interactive model** adds the importance of feedback loops; **cross-cultural communication** recognises that the sender's and receiver's cultures, generations, experiences and emotional states affect understanding.

**Gulf of execution and evaluation** (Norman): the gap between what a user intends and what the system allows, and between what the system shows and what the user understands — relevant for designing communications and products.

### Communication methods

| Method | Description | Examples | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Interactive** | Real-time, multidirectional exchange | Meetings, phone/video calls, instant messaging, workshops | Immediate understanding, complex or sensitive topics, decisions |
| **Push** | Sent to specific recipients; ensures distribution but not understanding | Letters, memos, emails, reports, voicemails, press releases | Targeted information that must reach people |
| **Pull** | Recipients access the content at their own discretion | Intranet, wikis, knowledge repositories, e-learning, dashboards | Large volumes of information or large audiences |

### Other communication dimensions

- **Formal** (reports, briefings, contracts) vs **informal** (emails, ad-hoc conversations).
- **Written** vs **oral**; **verbal** vs **non-verbal** (most of the meaning in face-to-face communication is carried non-verbally).
- **Internal** vs **external**; **official** vs **unofficial**; **hierarchical** (upward, downward) vs **horizontal** (peers).
- **Communication technology** choices depend on urgency, availability, ease of use, project environment (co-located vs virtual), sensitivity and confidentiality.

### Communication skills

- **Active listening** — paying full attention, paraphrasing, asking clarifying questions.
- **Feedback** — timely, specific, constructive.
- **Presentations**, **meeting management** (agenda, timebox, facilitation, minutes, action items).
- The **5 Cs of written communication**: correct grammar and spelling, concise expression, clear purpose and expression for the reader, coherent logical flow, controlling flow of words and ideas.

**Output:** the **communications management plan** — stakeholder communication requirements; information to be communicated (language, format, content, detail); reason for distribution; timeframe and frequency; person responsible; person authorised to release confidential information; recipients; methods and technologies; resources (time and budget); escalation process; glossary; flow charts; constraints (legislation, regulation, technology).

## 12.7 Manage Communications *(Executing)*

**Purpose:** ensure timely and appropriate collection, creation, distribution, storage, retrieval, management, monitoring and disposition of project information.

- Uses: communication technology, methods, skills, PMIS, **project reporting** (status, progress, forecasts), interpersonal skills, meetings.
- Output: **project communications** (performance reports, deliverables status, schedule progress, costs incurred, presentations).
- In adaptive teams: **information radiators** (big visible charts, task boards, burn charts), daily stand-ups, sprint reviews, transparent backlogs, team chat channels, **osmotic communication** in co-located teams.

## 12.8 Monitor Communications *(Monitoring and Controlling)*

**Purpose:** ensure the information needs of the project and its stakeholders are met. Check whether communications had the desired effect (Did people read and understand? Are stakeholders acting on it?). Techniques: stakeholder engagement assessment matrix, observation, surveys, feedback, meetings. Outputs: work performance information, change requests (e.g., to the communications plan).

> **Trap.** "Send more emails" is rarely the answer to a communication problem. Check **what** the stakeholder needs, **how** they prefer to receive it, and whether the communications plan should change.

## 12.9 Stakeholders in adaptive and hybrid projects

- The **product owner** represents stakeholders' interests and the business; stakeholders still participate directly in **sprint reviews**, backlog refinement and user research.
- **Frequent demonstrations** align expectations with reality better than documents.
- **Transparency** — anyone can see the backlog, the board and the burn charts.
- In hybrid projects, combine formal reporting for governance bodies with agile transparency for users and the team, and make sure **one consistent story** is told.

A project team grows from 8 to 12 members (the project manager is included in these counts). How many additional communication channels are created?
A) 38
B) 66
C) 28
D) 4
= A
? Channels = n(n − 1)/2. With 8 people: 28. With 12 people: 66. The increase is 66 − 28 = 38.

@@
A stakeholder has high power but low interest in the project. According to the power/interest grid, what is the appropriate strategy?
A) Keep satisfied
B) Manage closely
C) Keep informed
D) Monitor with minimum effort
= A
? High power, low interest stakeholders should be kept satisfied so they do not become obstacles. High–high is manage closely; low power–high interest is keep informed.

@@
Which three attributes are used in the salience model to classify stakeholders?
A) Power, urgency and legitimacy
B) Power, interest and influence
C) Impact, influence and interest
D) Urgency, budget and authority
= A
? The salience model uses power, urgency and legitimacy. Grids use pairs such as power/interest or impact/influence.

The stakeholder engagement assessment matrix shows the IT security manager as "Resistant" now and "Supportive" as desired. What should the project manager do next?
A) Plan engagement actions to understand and address the manager's concerns
B) Remove the IT security manager from the stakeholder register, since the manager is resistant
C) Escalate to the sponsor to override the manager
D) Change the desired level to "Resistant" to close the gap
= A
? The gap between current and desired engagement drives targeted engagement actions. Understanding the concerns is the first step to moving the stakeholder toward support.

A project manager needs to share a 300-page technical specification with 400 stakeholders worldwide who will consult it as needed. Which communication method is most appropriate?
A) Pull communication via a shared repository
B) Push communication by sending the file to everyone by email
C) Interactive communication in a single meeting
D) Informal hallway conversations
= A
? Pull communication suits large volumes of information and large audiences who access it at their discretion.

@@
An important decision about a scope trade-off must be made by several stakeholders who disagree. Which communication method is best?
A) Interactive communication, such as a facilitated meeting
B) Push communication, such as a detailed memo sent to everyone involved
C) Pull communication, such as a wiki page
D) A monthly newsletter
= A
? Complex or sensitive topics that require mutual understanding and decisions are best handled through real-time, multidirectional interactive communication.

In the basic communication model, who is responsible for confirming that the message was understood correctly?
A) The sender, by checking for feedback
B) Only the receiver
C) The medium
D) The project sponsor, who is accountable for all communication
= A
? The sender encodes the message clearly and confirms it was understood; the receiver decodes it, acknowledges receipt and provides feedback.
