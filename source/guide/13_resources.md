# Chapter 13 — Resources Performance Domain (including Procurement)

**After this chapter you should be able to:**
- describe the five Resources processes and their key outputs;
- plan team and physical resources with RAMs/RACI charts, team charters and resource breakdown structures;
- acquire internal and external resources, including the main steps of conducting a procurement;
- lead the team (development, performance, recognition) — the detailed people skills are in Chapter 16;
- monitor and control physical resources and contracts, including claims and incentive-fee calculations.

## 13.1 What the Resources domain covers

The Resources domain addresses **identifying, acquiring, leading and managing the people, physical and virtual resources** needed to complete the project. It covers the team *and* equipment, materials, facilities, software and infrastructure, and — together with *Plan Sourcing Strategy* in Governance and Appendix X4 — the acquisition and control of externally sourced resources.

| Process | Focus area | Key outputs |
|---|---|---|
| **Plan Resource Management** | Planning | Resource management plan, team charter |
| **Estimate Resources** | Planning | Resource requirements, basis of estimates, resource breakdown structure |
| **Acquire Resources** | Executing | Physical resource assignments, project team assignments, resource calendars, (for external sourcing) selected sellers and agreements |
| **Lead the Team** | Executing | Team performance assessments, change requests, updates (formerly *Develop Team* and *Manage Team*) |
| **Monitor and Control Resourcing** | Monitoring and Controlling | Work performance information, change requests (physical/virtual resources, and contracts) |

**Desired outcomes:** the right resources are available at the right time and place; the team is high-performing, engaged and growing; resources are used efficiently and sustainably; suppliers deliver as agreed.

## 13.2 Plan Resource Management *(Planning)*

**Resource management plan** contents: identification of resources; how to acquire them; **roles and responsibilities** (role, authority, responsibility, competence); project organisation charts; project team resource management (how to define, staff, manage and release); training; team development methods; resource control; recognition plan.

### Data representation tools

| Tool | Description |
|---|---|
| **Hierarchical charts** — organisational breakdown structure (OBS), resource breakdown structure (RBS), WBS | Show reporting relationships, resource categories and types |
| **Responsibility assignment matrix (RAM)** | Grid linking work packages/activities to people or groups |
| **RACI chart** | A RAM with **R**esponsible (does the work), **A**ccountable (owns the outcome — **only one A per task**), **C**onsulted (two-way input), **I**nformed (one-way updates) |
| **Text-oriented formats** | Position descriptions, role-responsibility-authority forms |

### Team charter

A document created **by the team** that establishes values, agreements and operating guidelines: team values, communication guidelines, **decision-making criteria and process**, **conflict resolution process**, meeting guidelines, team agreements (ground rules), working hours across time zones. The team charter is most effective when the team writes it together and revisits it.

## 13.3 Estimate Resources *(Planning)*

Estimate team resources and the type and quantities of materials, equipment and supplies. Techniques: bottom-up, analogous and parametric estimating, alternatives analysis (in-house vs outsource, manual vs automated, different skill levels), PMIS. Outputs: **resource requirements**, **basis of estimates**, **resource breakdown structure** (hierarchy of resources by category — labour, material, equipment, supplies — and type).

## 13.4 Acquire Resources *(Executing)*

**Purpose:** obtain the team members, facilities, equipment, materials, supplies and other resources needed — from inside the organisation or from external sources.

### Internal acquisition

- **Pre-assignment** — team members selected in advance (e.g., named in the proposal or charter).
- **Negotiation** — with functional managers, other project managers, external organisations. The PM's influence is critical in matrix organisations.
- **Virtual teams** — expand the talent pool, reduce travel, enable people with mobility limitations or in different time zones; require strong communication planning and technology.
- **Multicriteria decision analysis** — weigh availability, cost, ability, experience, knowledge, skills, attitude, international factors.
- **Halo effect** — assuming someone is good at everything because they are good at one thing (e.g., promoting the best developer to PM). Avoid it in selection.
- If preferred resources are not available, use **alternative resources** (with lower competence or higher cost), document the impact and consider risk.

### External acquisition — conducting a procurement

When resources or work are sourced externally, the steps are typically:

1. **Advertise** and distribute **bid documents** (RFI/RFQ/RFP, procurement SOW) to prospective sellers.
2. Hold a **bidder conference** (pre-bid or vendor conference) so all prospective sellers receive the **same information** at the same time — fairness and transparency. Questions and answers are shared with all bidders.
3. **Receive proposals** and evaluate them against the **source selection criteria** using a **weighting system**, **screening** (must-meet criteria), independent estimates, and expert review — often by a proposal evaluation committee.
4. **Negotiate** — clarify structure, requirements and terms; the **procurement/contracts department** usually leads, and the PM supports.
5. **Award** the **agreement** (contract) — a mutually binding legal agreement that obligates the seller to provide the specified products/services and the buyer to pay.

**Typical contract components:** statement of work or deliverables; schedule and milestones; performance reporting; period of performance; roles and responsibilities; seller's place of performance; pricing and payment terms; inspection, quality and acceptance criteria; **warranty**; product support; **limitation of liability**; fees and **retainage** (amount withheld until completion); **penalties** and **liquidated damages** (pre-agreed compensation for specific breaches such as late delivery); **incentives**; insurance and performance bonds; subcontractor approvals; **change request handling**; **termination clause** and **alternative dispute resolution (ADR)** mechanisms; **force majeure** (unforeseeable circumstances that excuse non-performance); **indemnification**; confidentiality and **intellectual property** provisions.

> **Exam tip.** A contract is a **legal** document: changes to it must follow the **contract change control system** and usually require the authority of the procurement/contracts administrator. The PM cannot change a contract by informal agreement with the seller's project manager. Also, the **buyer's PM should not direct the seller's employees** as if they were their own staff — work through the contract and the seller's management.

## 13.5 Lead the Team *(Executing)*

This process merges the former *Develop Team* (improving competencies, interaction and environment) and *Manage Team* (tracking performance, providing feedback, resolving issues, managing changes). Key ideas — expanded in Chapter 16:

- **Team development** — training, team-building, recognition and rewards, co-location (war rooms, *tight matrix*) or effective virtual collaboration, communication technology, individual and team assessments (personality and style assessments, 360° feedback).
- **Stages of team development (Tuckman):** forming, storming, norming, performing, adjourning.
- **Motivation** — intrinsic and extrinsic, autonomy-mastery-purpose; recognition should be **timely**, **specific** and appropriate to the person and culture; win-lose (zero-sum) rewards that only a few can earn can hurt team cohesion.
- **Team performance assessments** — evaluate improvements in skills, competencies, team behaviour, turnover rate, cohesiveness.
- **Conflict management**, **decision-making**, **emotional intelligence**, **influencing**, **leadership**.
- **Self-organising teams** in adaptive contexts; the leader is a **servant leader** who removes impediments, shields the team, coaches and facilitates.
- **Performance problems** — address them **privately**, early, with specific feedback, focusing on behaviour and support; involve the functional manager when appropriate (it may affect their appraisal).

## 13.6 Monitor and Control Resourcing *(Monitoring and Controlling)*

**Purpose:** ensure that the **physical and virtual resources** (equipment, materials, facilities, software, infrastructure) assigned and allocated are available as planned, monitor planned vs actual use, and take corrective action. It also covers monitoring **externally sourced** resources and contract performance. (Team performance itself is handled through *Lead the Team*.)

- Techniques: data analysis (alternatives, cost-benefit, performance reviews, trend analysis), problem solving, negotiation, influencing, PMIS.
- Physical resource issues: late deliveries, wrong quantities, defective materials, equipment failures, scarcity, price increases, environmental waste.
- **Just-in-time (JIT)** inventory reduces holding costs but requires reliable suppliers.

### Controlling contracts (procurement control)

- **Contract administration** — manage the relationship, monitor performance against the agreement, approve payments, manage changes.
- **Performance reviews** and **inspections/audits** — verify the seller's work and processes.
- **Earned value analysis** of seller performance; **trend analysis**.
- **Claims administration** — a **claim** (dispute, appeal) arises when buyer and seller cannot agree on compensation for a change or on whether a change occurred. Preferred resolution order: **negotiation** → **alternative dispute resolution (ADR)** such as **mediation** (a neutral third party helps the parties agree) or **arbitration** (a neutral third party decides; often binding) → **litigation** (courts) only as a last resort.
- **Records management** — maintain complete records of contract and procurement activities (they may be needed in disputes or audits).
- **Payment systems** — pay according to contract terms, upon verification of work.
- **Closing a contract** — see Chapter 7: verify deliverables accepted, settle claims, final payment, formal written notice of completion, archive records, lessons learned. A contract may also be terminated early **for cause** (breach) or **for convenience** (as allowed by the termination clause).

> **Trap.** If a seller is not performing, the first step is usually to **review the contract and the seller's performance data, then discuss with the seller** (through the formal channel) — not to terminate the contract or withhold all payments immediately.

## 13.7 Incentive contracts — calculations you may see

**Cost plus incentive fee (CPIF)** example: target cost $100,000; target fee $10,000; share ratio **80/20** (buyer/seller); actual cost **$90,000**.
- Underrun = $10,000; seller's share = 20% × 10,000 = $2,000.
- Fee = 10,000 + 2,000 = **$12,000**; total price paid = 90,000 + 12,000 = **$102,000**.
- If actual cost were $110,000: overrun $10,000; seller's share −$2,000; fee $8,000; total $118,000.

**Fixed price incentive fee (FPIF)** example: target cost $200,000; target profit $20,000 (target price $220,000); share ratio 70/30; **ceiling price $240,000**; actual cost $210,000.
- Overrun $10,000; seller absorbs 30% = $3,000; profit = 20,000 − 3,000 = $17,000; price = 210,000 + 17,000 = **$227,000** (below the ceiling, so payable).
- **Point of total assumption (PTA)** = ((ceiling price − target price) ÷ buyer's share ratio) + target cost = ((240,000 − 220,000) ÷ 0.70) + 200,000 ≈ **$228,571**. Above this cost, the seller bears **all** additional costs.

## 13.8 Resources in adaptive and hybrid projects

- Small (often 3–9 people), stable, **dedicated**, **cross-functional** teams; **T-shaped** people (deep in one skill, broad across others) and generalising specialists reduce bottlenecks.
- Teams are **self-organising**; work is pulled, not assigned.
- Co-location or strong virtual collaboration practices; **sustainable pace**.
- Vendors may join as part of blended teams under agile-friendly contracts (Chapter 7).
- In hybrid projects, physical resources for predictive components (heavy equipment, materials) are planned and controlled in the traditional way, while team capacity for adaptive components is managed through velocity and capacity planning.
