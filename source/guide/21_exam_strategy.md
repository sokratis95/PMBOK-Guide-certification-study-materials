# Chapter 21 — Exam Strategy and the PMI Mindset

**After this chapter you should be able to:**
- apply the "PMI mindset" to choose the best answer among several plausible options;
- use a repeatable technique to read and answer situational questions;
- manage time, breaks and stress on exam day;
- avoid the most common traps.

## 21.1 The PMI mindset — 25 rules of thumb

These are not laws, but they describe how the "best" answer is usually chosen.

**Leadership and people**
1. **Be a servant leader.** Support, coach, facilitate and remove impediments; don't command and control unless there is an emergency.
2. **Empower the team.** Let self-organising teams decide *how* to do their work; involve the team in estimates and plans.
3. **Talk to people directly.** Address problems face-to-face (or the closest equivalent) and privately, before escalating.
4. **Collaborate to resolve conflict.** Problem-solving is the default; forcing only for emergencies or ethics.
5. **Recognise and develop people.** Training, mentoring and recognition are good investments.
6. **Respect culture and diversity.** Adapt communication; include everyone.

**Analysis and action**
7. **Assess before acting.** When something happens, first understand it: gather facts, review the plan, analyse impact and root cause.
8. **But don't just analyse — act.** Among options, prefer the one that *does something* appropriate after analysis, rather than waiting or ignoring.
9. **Fix root causes, not symptoms.**
10. **Be proactive.** Anticipate risks, engage stakeholders early, plan communication, prevent problems.
11. **Prevention over inspection; quality is built in.**

**Process and governance**
12. **Follow the agreed process.** Changes to baselines go through change control; contracts through contract change control; agile changes through the backlog and product owner.
13. **Know who decides.** The product owner decides priorities; the sponsor/steering committee decides on business case and major changes; the CCB on changes per thresholds; the team on how to build. The PM facilitates and recommends.
14. **Escalate only when needed** — when the issue exceeds your authority or the team's ability, and after trying to resolve it yourself — and escalate with **options**, not just problems.
15. **Keep documents current.** Update the risk register, issue log, lessons learned, stakeholder register as things happen.
16. **Never implement unapproved changes**, and don't gold-plate.
17. **Close properly.** Formal acceptance, lessons learned, contract closure, archiving, then release resources.

**Value, ethics and environment**
18. **Focus on value and outcomes**, not just outputs or the triple constraint.
19. **Be honest and transparent.** Report bad news early with options; never hide problems or falsify data.
20. **Follow laws, regulations, policies and the Code of Ethics.** Disclose conflicts of interest; refuse bribes; report illegal conduct.
21. **Consider sustainability and the wider impact** of decisions.
22. **Use AI responsibly** — as an aid, with human validation, transparency and data protection.

**Context**
23. **Identify the approach** (predictive, agile, hybrid) and answer in that context.
24. **Tailor** — the right amount of process for the situation.
25. **Assume you are in a well-run organisation** with the usual artifacts (charter, plans, registers) unless the question says otherwise — and that the PM has the authority the question describes.

## 21.2 A repeatable technique for situational questions

1. **Read the last sentence first** — what is being asked? (*first*, *next*, *best*, *most likely*, *least*, *EXCEPT*, *what should have been done to prevent…*)
2. **Read the scenario and underline the key facts**: the approach (sprints? baseline?), the phase (planning, executing, closing?), who is involved, what has already been done, the real problem (often not the one stated).
3. **Predict the answer** before reading the options.
4. **Eliminate** clearly wrong options — usually two:
   - options that ignore the problem, delay without reason, or are passive;
   - options that escalate prematurely or hand the problem to someone else;
   - options that break rules (unapproved changes, ethics violations, bypassing the product owner);
   - options that use the wrong approach for the context (formal CCB for a backlog item in Scrum; "just start coding" in a regulated predictive project).
5. **Choose between the remaining two** by asking: *Which one happens first? Which one addresses the root cause? Which one is more aligned with servant leadership, value and the agreed process?*
6. **Watch for "first" vs "next".** "First" usually means analysis or understanding (review, assess, discuss). "Next" assumes something has already been done — read carefully what that is.
7. **Don't add facts.** Answer the question as written, not the situation you imagine.
8. **Flag and move on** if you're stuck for more than ~2 minutes. Never leave a question blank — there is no penalty for guessing.

## 21.3 Typical question patterns and their usual answers

| Pattern | Usually best |
|---|---|
| A stakeholder asks for a change in a predictive project | Assess impact → change request → CCB decision → update plan → communicate |
| A stakeholder asks for a change in an agile project | Add to the backlog; product owner prioritises |
| A team member is underperforming | Private conversation to understand causes; coaching/training; then involve functional manager if needed |
| Two team members are in conflict | Let them try first; then facilitate a collaborative discussion |
| The sponsor wants a delivery date the team believes is impossible | Analyse with the team; present data and options (scope, resources, phasing); negotiate |
| A risk occurs that was identified | Implement the planned response (risk owner); update the register |
| An unidentified problem occurs | Treat it as an issue: assess, workaround, issue log, then identify new risks |
| A new stakeholder appears | Add to the stakeholder register, analyse, plan engagement |
| The team is missing sprint commitments | Discuss in the retrospective; look at capacity, WIP, impediments, estimation |
| A vendor is late | Review the contract and performance; communicate with the vendor through the agreed channel |
| The project is cancelled | Close the project: document, transfer, close contracts, lessons learned, release resources |
| A team member asks the PM to decide a technical approach (agile) | Coach the team to decide |
| The product owner is unavailable | Coach on the role; agree availability; escalate if needed |
| A regulatory change appears mid-project | Assess impact with experts; process changes; compliance is mandatory |
| A request to hide bad news | Refuse; report transparently with options |

## 21.4 Time management and exam day

- **Pacing:** ~80 seconds per question. Checkpoints: Q60 at ~80 min, Q120 at ~160 min, Q180 at ~235 min.
- **Breaks:** take both. Before each break, review flagged questions in that section — you can't return after the break.
- **Calculations:** write the formula, then plug in numbers. Re-check signs (negative CV = over budget).
- **Case-study sets:** read the case once carefully, then refer back for each question; the questions may test different domains.
- **Multiple-response:** the question tells you how many to choose; partial credit is not given.
- **Matching/drag-and-drop:** start with the pairs you are sure of; use elimination for the rest.
- **Online proctored exams:** test your system in advance; clear your desk; have ID ready; no notes, phones or watches.
- **Physical readiness:** sleep, hydration, a light meal. Arrive early (or log in early).
- **Mental readiness:** expect hard questions — the exam feels difficult for almost everyone. Many unscored pretest items are deliberately difficult; don't let them shake you.

## 21.5 The final two weeks

- Take at least **three full-length timed simulations** (this package has ten).
- Review **every** explanation, especially where you chose the second-best answer — those reveal mindset gaps.
- Keep an **error log**: question, your answer, correct answer, why, which principle or process it tests.
- Re-read Chapters 4 (principles), 7 (governance/change), 15 (agile), 16 (people) and this chapter.
- Memorise the formula sheet (Chapter 20) and practise ten calculations per day.
- Stop heavy studying the day before the exam; review the mindset rules and rest.

A question asks what the project manager should do FIRST when a team member reports that a critical supplier may deliver late. Which type of answer is most likely to be correct?
A) An action that gathers information and assesses the situation first
B) Terminating the supplier's contract
C) Escalating immediately to the sponsor
D) Ignoring the report until the delay has been confirmed in writing by the supplier's management
= A
? "First" usually points to understanding the situation and its impact before taking larger actions such as escalation or termination.

@@
In an agile project, a senior manager asks the project manager to add a feature to the current sprint. What is the best response?
A) Explain that the product owner prioritises new work, and connect them
B) Add the feature to the current sprint immediately
C) Refuse the request because the sprint is locked
D) Submit the request to the change control board, since all scope changes need formal approval
= A
? In agile contexts, the product owner prioritises backlog items. The PM facilitates the connection and protects the sprint goal rather than refusing outright.

@@
Which answer pattern is usually WRONG on the PMP exam?
A) Escalating to the sponsor before trying to understand or resolve the problem
B) Meeting privately with a team member to discuss the concerns about their recent performance
C) Updating the risk register when new risks are identified
D) Facilitating a collaborative discussion to resolve conflict
= A
? Premature escalation is a common wrong answer. Escalation is appropriate when an issue exceeds the PM's authority or the team's ability, ideally with options.

@@
Before taking the first optional break, what should a candidate do?
A) Review the flagged questions in the first section, since they cannot be revisited
B) Skip remaining questions to save time
C) Leave the flagged questions to review at the end of the exam, when there is more time
D) Nothing; breaks have no effect on navigation
= A
? Once a break begins, the previous section is locked. Reviewing flagged questions beforehand prevents losing the chance to change answers.

The sponsor asks the project manager to report the project as "green" to the board although it is significantly behind schedule. What should the project manager do?
A) Report the true status, with recovery options, and discuss the concern with the sponsor
B) Report green as requested
C) Report green as requested, but tell the team the truth so that they can recover the delay
D) Avoid sending any report this month
= A
? Honesty is a mandatory standard of the Code of Ethics. The PM reports accurately and provides options, while working with the sponsor.
